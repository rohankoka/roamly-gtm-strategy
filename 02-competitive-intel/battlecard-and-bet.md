# Competitive Intelligence & Strategic Bet · Roamly Groups

## 1. Primary competitor

- **Competitor:** Viator
- **Core offering:** A marketplace where friends can discover, compare, and book tours and local experiences for their group, with a wide selection and flexible booking options.
- **How they position themselves:** Viator positions itself as a trusted place to find and book memorable experiences, emphasizing **wide choice, traveler reviews, and flexible booking** to help travelers book with confidence.
- **Primary audience:** Viator’s primary audience is leisure travelers—including individuals, couples, families, and friends—looking to discover and book tours, activities, and attractions
- **Known pricing or packaging:** Pay per experience, with prices varying by activity, booking option, and group size. Eligible experiences offer Reserve Now, Pay Later at no additional cost.
For groups, this lets one organizer reserve for everyone and collect money from friends later; it differs from each friend paying their share directly toward the booking
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** Viator is Roamly Groups’ biggest threat because friends can already use it to find and book experiences together. Its wide selection, traveler reviews, and flexible booking options give them reasons to choose it. Roamly Groups needs to show how much easier it makes planning together and splitting payments to win that booking

## 2. Battlecard

### Competitive Battlecard: Roamly Groups vs. Viator

**Version:** V1 · Internal working draft  
**Target segment:** Friends traveling together  
**Positioning:** Easier group coordination, booking, and payment.

> **Evidence note:** Roamly Groups’ capabilities are proposed. Potential gaps in Viator’s group workflow require validation.

#### 1. Know the Landscape

| Field | Viator |
|---|---|
| **Competitor overview** | An experience marketplace competing for the same group activity booking. |
| **Primary audience** | Leisure travelers, including friends, couples, families, and individuals. |
| **Key offerings** | Tours, activities, attractions, traveler reviews, and flexible booking options. |
| **Pricing** | Pay per experience; prices vary by activity, booking option, and group size. |
| **Payment options** | Reserve Now, Pay Later at no additional cost on eligible experiences. [www.viator.com](https://www.viator.com/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) |

#### 2. Understand the Battlefield

##### Key Strengths

- **Broad selection:** More options across destinations.
- **Traveler reviews:** Help customers evaluate unfamiliar experiences.
- **Booking flexibility:** Eligible reservations offer deferred payment and flexible cancellation.
- **Familiarity:** Existing Viator customers have less reason to try another platform.

##### Key Weaknesses and Openings

- **Organizer workload:** Viator describes one person reserving for everyone and collecting money afterward. [partnerresources.viator.com](https://partnerresources.viator.com/blog/reservenowpaylater/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)
- **Payment timing versus payment splitting:** Deferred payment alone does not solve collecting each friend’s share.
- **Group coordination:** Shared decisions and itinerary management are opportunities to investigate, rather than confirmed Viator gaps.

#### 3. Win the Deal

| Field | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Why we win** | Coordinated booking, split payments, and a shared itinerary can reduce the organizer’s work. |
| **Why they win** | Selection, destination coverage, reviews, price, or availability of a specific experience. |
| **Why choose Roamly Groups?** | “Book a local experience with friends without managing separate bookings or chasing everyone for money.” |
| **Best-fit customer** | An organizer struggling to coordinate friends and collect payments. |
| **Poor-fit customer** | A group whose priority is an experience unavailable on Roamly, or whose current booking process already works well. |

##### Defensibility

**Features alone are copyable.** A stronger advantage would combine group-ready hosts, reliable availability, participant payments, clear refund rules, and an easy invitation flow.

Roamly’s vetted host network and strong repeat booking provide a foundation; the pilot must prove the group experience delivers.

#### 4. Handle the Hard Moments

##### Quick Dismiss

“If your group has already agreed, one person is happy to pay, and Viator has the experience you want, it may meet your needs. Roamly Groups is designed for groups where coordination and collecting money create extra work.”

##### Landmine Questions

- Who pays upfront and follows up on reimbursements?
- Can each friend pay directly toward the same booking?
- What happens to availability while everyone pays?
- What happens if one participant cancels?
- Where does everyone find the latest confirmed itinerary?

**Internal check:** Roamly must answer these questions clearly too.

##### Objection Handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| **“Viator has more choices.”** | “That’s a strength. When Roamly has the experience you want, our value is easier coordination and payment.” |
| **“Viator already offers pay later.”** | “That helps with payment timing. Roamly Groups aims to let each friend pay their share directly.” |
| **“WhatsApp and bank transfers work.”** | “They can. Groups becomes useful when tracking plans and payments across tools becomes a burden.” |
| **“Is Roamly cheaper?”** | “We haven’t established a price advantage. Compare equivalent experiences, inclusions, and terms.” |
| **“Why trust a new feature?”** | “The pilot needs to demonstrate reliable bookings and clear payment, cancellation, and refund rules.” |

#### 5. Claims to Avoid

- “Viator has no group booking functionality.”
- “Viator cannot support split payments” without verification.
- “Roamly is cheaper” without a like-for-like comparison.
- “Roamly eliminates every group planning problem.”

#### 6. Validate Before Launch

- Test Viator’s participant payment and group coordination flows.
- Confirm Roamly’s availability, payment deadline, and cancellation rules.
- Test whether organizers experience less work.
- Measure whether Groups generates additional booking value.

## 3. What I want to revisit in my Module 1 strategy

I want to revisit my value proposition because the battlecard shows that friends can already book experiences together through Viator. I need to make it clearer why Roamly Groups is easier for the organizer—especially when coordinating plans and collecting everyone’s share—and test whether that benefit is strong enough to change how they book.

## 4. Strategic bet

### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** I’m doubling down on friends traveling together, but narrowing the focus to existing Roamly customers who organize experiences for their friend group.
- **The one thing worth choosing it for:** Less work for the group organizer. Roamly Groups brings booking, split payments, and a shared itinerary together, so the person planning the experience doesn’t have to manage separate bookings or chase friends for money. That’s the clearest reason to choose it over Viator
- **The biggest threat to that position:** The biggest threat is that Viator could make group coordination and split payments just as easy, weakening our main reason to choose Roamly Groups

### Insight
The signal that changes my thinking most is that people are already doing the work of group booking themselves. They’re coordinating accounts, schedules, and payments because they want the experience together. That suggests an opportunity to make the organizer’s job easier.

Viator wins on choice, reviews, and booking flexibility. To win that customer, Roamly Groups needs to show that the whole group can get from “let’s do this” to a confirmed booking with less effort. Our message should focus on that relief, and the product needs to deliver it.

### Belief
I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when it makes organizing an experience with friends easier, because the person planning it has less work to do—from coordinating everyone’s plans to collecting their share of the payment

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by focusing first on existing customers who plan experiences for their friends, making it easy to coordinate, book, and split payments so they can enjoy the experience without doing all the organizing work themselves.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** To make this bet, I am deprioritizing corporate teams, large celebration groups, and helping solo travelers find a group to join. I’m focusing first on existing customers planning experiences with friends, so we can solve their coordination and payment frustrations before expanding to other audiences.
