# Pricing Recommendation · Roamly Groups

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Grow revenue from a known audience

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based
- **Who pays, and for what:** Each friend pays for their own place in a confirmed group experience. Group coordination, split payments, and the shared itinerary are included in the booking.
For this , I’d assume Roamly earns a commission on completed bookings, with no separate Groups fee. This supports the Expand goal by making it easier for existing customers to book with friends.

## 3. Packaging

- **Tiers:**
  - Good: Groups Basic — Friends booking one experience together — Experience price, $0 Groups fee.
  - Better ⭐: Groups Plus — Organizers coordinating several experiences on one trip — Experience prices + approximately $10 per group per trip.
  - Best: Groups Concierge — Organizers wanting personal planning assistance — Experience prices + approximately $49 per group per trip.

## 4. Assumption to test

- **The one assumption I would test before launch:** I’d test whether group organizers are willing to pay around $10 per group per trip for Groups Plus when basic group booking and split payments are already included for free. If they don’t see enough added value to pay, I’d rethink the paid tiers and focus on growing revenue through completed bookings.

## 5. One-page recommendation

### Roamly Groups · V1 Pricing Recommendation
**For Finance, Sales Leadership and Product Marketing**

#### Recommendation

Launch **Groups Basic with no additional Groups fee**. Test paid upgrades before introducing them.

The goal is to **grow revenue from existing customers** who organize experiences with friends. Including group booking, split payments and a shared itinerary supports our strategic bet—reduce the organizer’s workload so more groups complete bookings.

#### Model and Proposed Packages

Each participant pays for their own place in the experience. This recommendation assumes Roamly earns commission on completed bookings, subject to Finance confirming the commercial model.

| Package | Customer need | Proposed price | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Groups Basic** | Book an experience with friends | Experience price + **$0 Groups fee** | Launch with invitations, split payments and shared itinerary |
| **Groups Plus ⭐** | Coordinate several experiences on one trip | Experience prices + **~$10 per group per trip** | Test as the intended preferred upgrade once additional benefits are defined |
| **Groups Concierge** | Get personal planning assistance | Experience prices + **~$49 per group per trip** | Validate demand and service costs before offering |

**The paid tiers remain hypotheses.** Plus needs a clear benefit beyond Basic, and Concierge introduces an operational service outside the current feature scope.

#### What Each Stakeholder Most Needs to Hear

**Finance — prove profitable expansion.**  
Measure incremental contribution per eligible existing customer against a control group. Include commission revenue, payment processing, refunds and support costs. Count individual and group bookings together to detect bookings simply moving into the new flow. Assess Concierge against the cost of staff time.

**Sales Leadership — keep adoption self-serve.**  
The initial buyer is an existing customer organizing for friends. Make the package and total cost easy to explain, with each participant’s share visible. Human assistance should be reserved for a validated Concierge offer with clear service limits.

**Product Marketing — protect the core promise.**  
Lead with less work for the organizer and direct payment by each friend. Keep those benefits accessible in Basic. Position upgrades around specific additional help, and use pilot evidence before claiming time savings or better booking outcomes.

#### Launch Decision

Proceed with Basic once payment, availability, cancellation and refund rules are reliable. Introduce Plus only when a defined package shows sufficient demand and positive economics. Revisit the tier structure if organizers consistently prefer Basic.

#### Research Method — Conjoint

Use **choice-based conjoint** to test whether organizers choose a clearly defined Plus package at around $10 per group per trip when Basic is available at no extra charge. Vary benefits and prices, and include Basic and a “book another way” option.

Conjoint best tests the **trade-off between added benefits and price**. Define Plus’s benefits first, then validate the survey findings through a live pricing pilot.
