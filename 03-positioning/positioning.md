# Positioning Framework & Statement · Roamly Groups

## 1. Positioning framework

- **For (target audience):** For existing Roamly customers who take the lead in planning experiences with friends and want an easier way to coordinate schedules, book together, and collect everyone’s share without managing it all themselves.
- **Who want / need:** I want to book something fun with my friends without being the one who has to coordinate everyone’s schedules, manage separate bookings, and chase people for money
- **We offer:** We offer a group booking feature that brings local experience bookings, split payments, and shared itinerary planning together in one place.
- **Because of (value proposition):** Helps group organizers spend less time coordinating plans and collecting money, so they can enjoy the experience alongside their friends.
- **Despite (barriers):** Despite a smaller selection and reach than Viator, and an unproven group booking flow, we need to give organizers a strong enough reason to move away from familiar booking tools and group chats.
- **Better than (differentiation):** For existing Roamly customers organizing experiences with friends, we build on a service they already know by bringing planning, booking, and payments together—reducing the work they currently manage across separate tools. Our advantage depends on delivering that complete journey reliably.

## 2. Positioning statement

### First draft
Roamly Groups brings local experience bookings, split payments, and shared itineraries into one place for existing Roamly customers planning with friends. It takes the work of managing separate bookings and chasing payments off the organizer, so they can enjoy the experience too.

### Pressure test
- **Test I ran:** A · Audience recognition
- **What it revealed:** The test suggested that “chasing payments” was the most relatable part because it names a real frustration. The opening felt more like a feature list, and “existing Roamly customers” sounded like internal targeting language. “Shared itineraries” didn’t clearly explain how coordination gets easier, while “takes the work off the organizer” overpromised. I need to lead with the organizer’s frustration and be more specific about the work Roamly Groups reduces.

### Final statement
Roamly Groups lets the friend who usually organizes everything book local experiences for the group, lets everyone pay their share directly, and keep the itinerary in one place. That means fewer separate bookings to manage and no need to collect everyone’s money, leaving the organizer more time to enjoy the experience too.
